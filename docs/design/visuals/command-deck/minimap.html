Design proposal. Not approved (DEC-VISUALS open). Sample data only. Nothing here replaces application code. Identity tokens (A1, E1, names) and the minimap picture are neutral placeholders, not game artwork.

What the picture is

  • The centre of the deck is the real in-game minimap, captured by the capture pipeline and shown enlarged. It is not redrawn from GSI coordinates. The owner's target is about 30 frames per second so that it does not stutter like a 1 Hz poll.
  • 30 fps is a target, not a measurement. The badge in the corner always states the real capture rate and the age of the frame on screen, so a slower pipeline is visible instead of hidden.
  • The overlay only annotates what the picture already contains. Markers are outlines around icons, not replacement icons, so the picture carries the smooth motion and a marker that is a frame late does not jump.
  • The picture in these mockups is drawn with CSS gradients. The four drifting specks stand for “this is live video”; they stop under reduced motion. They are picture content, not overlay markers.

Layers, bottom to top

  1. Captured frame, inset by a constant 10u gutter. The gutter never changes, so the picture never moves.
  2. Sighting trails.
  3. Markers and their label plates (dark plate, 88% opaque, so labels survive any picture).
  4. Frame: solid = live, dashed = Lite or degraded, striped hazard = critical.
  5. Critical banner, anchored top-left, at most 70% wide. Rate badge, bottom-right.
  6. Degraded-state plate, centred. When it is up, layers 2 and 3 are removed.

Rate and age badge

Filled dot = live at the target rate. Half dot and dashed border = Lite. Hollow dot = no new frame. The numbers are capture rate and the age of the displayed frame. Minimum size 20u.

Why those two corners

The banner and the badge sit in the top-left and bottom-right corners. The other two corners are left clear in every state. The assumption that those are the corners a player least wants covered needs a check against the real picture.

Markers

Only markers the baseline admits. No predicted positions, no wards, no objectives, no “probably here” zones, no enemy vitals.

States

Every state keeps the same box. The frame, the badge and a worded plate change together, so no state depends on colour alone.

The runtime-status contract today has capture.state = available | unsupported | unavailable. “Calibrating”, “Lite”, “stale” and “recalibration needed” need explicit states or reason codes before this can be built; see DESIGN-NOTES, open decisions.
